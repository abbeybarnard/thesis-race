    How it works

    What gets shared about me?

    Only your total word count and the date it changed, plus the bits you choose for the leaderboard: your display name, astronaut, mission patch emoji, nickname, and your submission date and notes if you add them. Your thesis itself, and its title, never leave your computer or Overleaf.

    Can anyone see my thesis or my private repo?

    No, not Abbey and not anyone else. The race never gets access to your files or your repo. If your thesis is on GitHub, the counting runs inside your own repo, using a key that you make and keep in your own repo's secrets, and that key can only post one tiny file holding your word count. The repo name you type on this page stays in your browser and is never sent anywhere.

    The reporter your repo runs is pinned to one exact version of the race's code, so nobody can change what runs in your repo later. You can read every line of it on GitHub. The VS Code script works the same way: it counts on your computer and sends only the total.

    Who can see the race?

    Anyone with the link can see this page. The race lives on GitHub, so your join entry and the numbers in the race's data files are public there too. Use a nickname as your display name if you'd rather not use your real name.

    Where does my word count come from?

    If you count by hand, it's whatever you type into Log my words, usually Overleaf's Menu → Word Count. If you set up automatic reporting, texcount counts your thesis on your own computer, or in your own GitHub repo, and sends just the total. Either way, headings and captions count, and the leaderboard catches up within three hours.

    How do I change my name, astronaut or submission date?

    Fill in the join form again with your new details and press Create. It updates your entry rather than adding a second one, and you won't need approving again.

    Do weekends break my streak?

    No. Weekends are rest days. Writing on a Saturday adds to your streak, but skipping one never breaks it.

    Can I take a break from the leaderboard?

    Yes. Quiet mode hides you from the orbit, the leaderboard, the crystal ball and the trophies. Your words still quietly count towards the group total and the pub, and your count keeps updating, so nothing is lost when you come back. (Your numbers do stay in the race's public data files on GitHub.)
